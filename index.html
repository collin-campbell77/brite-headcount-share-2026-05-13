Framing

We have a 2026 plan that targets ~$14M Brite Nites + ~$2M Brite Labs = ~$16M install-season revenue. The Labs side is the wildcard — sales pipeline confidence varies, the operational model is still maturing, and we haven't formally planned what the org looks like under different Labs outcomes.

This memo lays out the data inquiries we need to resolve and a rough outline of what the 2026 season looks like for each of you under three Labs sales conditions:

  • Condition 1 — Labs doesn't scale. Sales succeeds at Nites but Labs revenue lands at near zero ($0–0.5M). Most projects route through Nites residential and Tier C commercial. Labs ops contracts.
  • Condition 2 — Plan hits. Current 2026 plan baseline. ~$14M Nites + ~$2M Labs. Labs at ~12% mix. ~20 Labs projects (~50/50 between Tier A "full production" and Tier B "specialty pop-ups").
  • Condition 3 — Labs grows aggressively. Sales overperforms on Labs specifically — Gaylord expansion, municipal RFP wins, more Tier B closes. Labs at 20–25% mix ($3–4M). Nites side roughly steady.

The goal of this meeting is to (a) align on the data we need to pull to make this model decision-useful and (b) sketch how each of our roles shifts under each condition — so we know what to plan for, what to hedge against, and what to leave open until the season tells us which condition we're in.

Data Inquiries

What the Model Needs

The headcount model is only as good as the data feeding it. Below is what the model needs, what we have today, and what's still open.

1.A — The data the model depends on

The framework is built on a small set of inputs:

  • Per-crew throughput — $/crew/day, $/crew/season for Brite Nites exterior holiday work. Calibrates the formula for how much revenue each Nites field crew produces.
  • Labs project pipeline detail — project count, average duration by tier, geographic distribution. Drives the concurrency-based math for Labs supervisor demand.
  • Mix of revenue by tier — what portion of total sales is Tier A (full production, Labs Sup dedicated), Tier B (specialty commercial, Labs Sup required), Tier C (straightforward commercial, Nites motion).
  • Pipeline-stage forecasts — committed-deals-only and full-weighted-pipeline revenue, by record type and territory, so the model can output both a "floor" and "ceiling" headcount estimate.

1.C — Open data decisions for the meeting

These don't have to be locked at the brainstorm, but they should be on the agenda. Everything below is a group discussion item — individuals bring perspective from their domain, the call gets made together.

  1. Brite Base aggregation path. Three options: build a dashboard inside Brite Base for per-crew revenue (TM-useful side benefit, heaviest lift); periodic CSV export refreshed quarterly (cheapest viable); hand-feed constants (cheapest, but drifts). For v1 the leaning is periodic export — but who builds and owns it is open.
  2. Salesforce-to-calculator integration shape. Weekly export of every active deal with (record type, stage, probability weight, contract value, territory, expected install date) is the most flexible data shape. The calculator aggregates whatever slice it needs. Need to align on Salesforce admin capacity and timeline.
  3. Tier tagging at deal creation. Should AEs add an explicit Tier A/B/C field on Labs deals at quote time, or should the calculator infer tier from deal value bands ($100K+ = A, $20–100K = B, <$20K = C)? Tagging is cleaner; inferring is faster to launch. Worth a group conversation.
  4. Noel Decor reporting maturity. Current state of Noel Decor job logging in Brite Base is unclear, and may need investment before the interior side of the model can be calibrated. Group needs to align on whether this gets investment now or sits as a known gap.
  5. Labs project pipeline visibility. Even rough numbers against the 2026 pipeline — committed vs probable vs stretch projects, with tier classification and territory — would ground the concurrency model better than the current placeholders. Group to discuss the right level of detail to commit to.
  6. Deferred to a future meeting: Per-territory layer (each of 14 territories has its own capacity and headcount needs — adds a third dimension to the model after the general framework is locked); permanent smart lighting installs (bracketed as a separate entity per earlier discussion); equipment fleet planning (MEWPs, scissor lifts, booms — capex scales with Labs Tier A/B growth and deserves its own workstream).

Sales-Success Conditions

The Three Conditions

The 2026 plan calls for ~$16M install-season revenue split as ~$14M Nites + ~$2M Labs. The Nites side is well-understood and reasonably predictable. Labs is the variable. The three conditions below define what range of Labs outcomes we want the org plan to handle.

The conditions are not equally likely. The middle one is the plan-hits scenario; the others are downside and upside contingencies for the Labs portion specifically. Nites revenue stays roughly steady across all three.

Condition 1 · Downside
Labs Doesn't Scale
Sales succeeds at the Nites side, but the Labs pipeline doesn't convert at the expected rate. Labs revenue lands at near zero ($0–0.5M, ~0–3% mix). Most commercial work routes through Tier C — straightforward outdoor lighting on commercial sites, executed by Nites motion. A handful of Tier B pop-ups may still close opportunistically but Tier A full-production work (Gaylord-scale) doesn't materialize.

What this looks like in the field: ~5 small Labs jobs all season. No need for a dedicated Labs producer hire. Existing leadership (Collin, Rainer, Erwin, AM backup) easily covers what little Labs supervision is needed.

Signal you'd see by: Late September. If the Tier A pipeline is empty heading into late September and the bar/restaurant Tier B inbound hasn't materialized, this is the condition we're operating in. The implication is significant: by the time we know we're in Condition 1, the install season has started. The Collin role pivot toward production development on the Nites side (Section 3) would emerge mid-season as Labs ops capacity becomes available, rather than being pre-planned.
Condition 2 · Plan Hits
Current 2026 Baseline
The published 2026 plan baseline. ~$14M Nites + ~$2M Labs = ~$16M total. Labs at ~12% mix. ~20 Labs projects across the season, split roughly 50/50 between Tier A (full production, ~$150K avg, Labs Supervisor dedicated 1–2 weeks per project) and Tier B (specialty commercial pop-ups, ~$50K avg, Labs Supervisor required but parallelizable at 2 jobs/week comfortable).

What this looks like in the field: Labs supervisor function covered by Collin + Rainer + Erwin + AM help, no new dedicated supervisor hire needed. Producer role (the formal Labs Supervisor hire) is on the table but optional for 2026; lean target is 3–4 Labs-exclusive year-round FTE total (current 2 + producer + maybe second sales rep).

Signal you'd see by: End of September. Tier A commitments locked in and Tier B inbound steady through the early-season window. By end of September the picture is clear enough to staff the rest of the season with confidence.
Condition 3 · Upside
Labs Grows Aggressively
Sales overperforms on Labs specifically. Gaylord expansion converts multiple properties; municipal RFP wins land; Tier B pop-ups close at 1.5–2× plan volume. Labs at 20–25% mix ($3–4M Labs revenue). ~35–40 Labs projects across the season; Tier A count alone doubles or more.

What this looks like in the field: Existing-leadership Labs supervisor coverage tops out. Need at peak 3–4 dedicated Labs Supervisors; leadership-team capacity (Collin + Rainer + Erwin) can no longer absorb the concurrency. Producer hire becomes a definite, not optional; second producer or AE may also be triggered. Equipment fleet planning becomes acute (more MEWPs / scissor lifts deployed simultaneously). AM cross-training into Labs supervision becomes a real lever.

Signal you'd see by: August. When the early-locking Tier A pipeline is visible. Lead time for a producer hire is real — if Condition 3 is materializing, the producer search would ideally have been kicked off by May/June, but August is the latest the org can commit to a Condition 3 staffing posture and still react in time.

Seasonal Outlines per Position × Condition

What Each Role Looks Like

For each of the 3 conditions, a paragraph-per-role sketch of what the 2026 season would look like. These are intentionally rough — the goal is to give the brainstorm a starting shape, not pre-decide who does what.

The roles covered are Collin, Rainer, Holden, Jaime, and Mike. Erwin appears as a supporting figure in the supervisor-coverage discussion but is not a primary role-holder for this conversation.

A note on Collin's role
Collin's position at Brite is a new role being developed — not an existing title. Functionally it splits into two seasonal modes: during the season, Collin runs Brite Labs operations (parallel to Mike's Operations Manager role for Nites). Off-season, Collin contributes to production development for both Labs and Nites — process improvement, training program build, cert pipeline maintenance, off-season project execution. Industry-adjacent comparators for context (not titles to assign): Director of Production Operations at an experiential design-build firm; Director of Field Training & Development at a seasonal-workforce field service company; Field Operations Manager with a training mandate at a multi-territory contractor. The exact shape of the role under each condition is part of what this brainstorm aims to define.
A note on Mike's role
Mike is Operations Manager across all three conditions. Title and core scope don't shift — Nites operations leadership, three-branch coordination, the November-overlap orchestration. What shifts is how much Labs-branch attention his role demands and which sub-functions get co-led with Collin.

Operations Manager. Pre-season focused on TM + AM hiring (May–July) and aligning the training program rollout with Holden's hiring waves. Open Questions for the Meeting

What This Brainstorm Needs to Put on the Table

Items below are the things this brainstorm needs to put on the table. None of these need to be resolved in one session — but each should at least surface and get an owner or follow-up step.

Role definition
1 Collin's role at Brite. The role we've been describing — Labs operations during the season, cross-entity production development off-season — isn't formally defined yet. The group needs to align on a title, scope, reporting line, and how the role flexes across the three conditions. If Condition 1 plays out, the role weight shifts toward production development for Nites; if Condition 3, it consolidates around Labs operations leadership at higher scale. Worth getting on the same page about what the role's center of gravity is and where it stretches.
Data flow and tooling
2 Brite Base aggregation. Per-job data exists; aggregated view doesn't. Group decision needed on whether to build a dashboard inside Brite Base, run periodic CSV exports, or hand-feed the constants. Recommendation for v1: periodic export refreshed quarterly. Owner of the export build is open.
3 Salesforce → calculator integration. Weekly export of all active deals with stage, record type, probability, value, territory, and install date is the proposed data shape. Need confirmation that this is operationally feasible and an owner for the build.
4 Tier tagging at deal creation. Explicit Tier A/B/C field on Labs deals (clean, longer to launch) vs. value-band inference in the calculator ($100K+ = A; $20–100K = B; <$20K = C). Group call.
5 Noel Decor reporting state. Currently unclear; may need investment before the interior side of the model can be calibrated. Jaime has the broader view; group decides whether to invest now or accept the gap.
6 Labs pipeline numbers. Rough committed / probable / stretch project counts by tier and territory, with even gut-level confidence levels. Rainer's pipeline knowledge is the foundation; group reviews and discusses.
Operational readiness
7 AM cross-training as Labs supervisor backup. In Condition 2 this is light (one or two AMs picking up overflow). In Condition 3 it becomes a real workstream — formal cross-training of multiple AMs into Labs supervision so the producer hire isn't a single point of failure. When does the program start, who designs it, and at what cost?
8 Lift cert pipeline budget. Brite-funded per earlier decision. Open: per-installer cert cost, who owns the program (Collin? Holden? a vendor relationship), how it integrates with the recruiting calendar's Wave 1 (cert-required installers ready by early September).
Triggers and signals
9 Condition signal dates. The proposal: Condition 1 confirmable by late September, Condition 2 by end of September, Condition 3 by August. Group should sanity-check these against how Salesforce pipeline visibility actually evolves through summer; later signals mean longer maintained optionality, which is expensive.
10 Producer hire trigger. Latest the producer search could start and still meet a Condition 3 staffing posture: May–June. If the August signal is the gating moment, the producer search has to be pre-emptive — meaning the group has to make a Condition 3 readiness commitment before knowing for sure. How much pre-emptive cost is the group willing to absorb?
Deferred to future conversations (flagged for visibility)
  • Per-territory layer — each of the 14 territories has its own capacity, season length, and headcount needs. Once the general framework is stable, the model gets a third dimension here. Probably its own dedicated meeting.
  • Permanent smart lighting installs — bracketed as a separate entity for this model. Worth raising at a follow-up meeting once Labs and Nites operational interplay is well-defined.
  • Equipment fleet planning — MEWPs, scissor lifts, towable booms. Capex scales with Labs Tier A/B growth (acute under Condition 3). Belongs in its own workstream, likely with Tony Gomez owning.
  • Long-term Labs Supervisor / producer dedicated hires — beyond 2026, as Labs concurrency grows past 3-person leadership capacity, dedicated hires become non-optional. Worth a 2027 planning conversation.
