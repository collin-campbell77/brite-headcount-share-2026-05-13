Framing

We have a 2026 plan that targets ~$14M Brite Nites + ~$2M Brite Labs = ~$16M install-season revenue. Both numbers are variable. Brite Nites itself can land anywhere in the ~$12M–$16M+ range based on Nites sales performance. Brite Labs can land anywhere in the ~$0–$4M+ range based on Labs sales performance. Total revenue moves across a meaningful band, and the org structure has to flex with both axes — scale (total revenue) AND mix (Labs share).

This memo lays out the data inquiries we need to resolve, the formula the org uses to translate revenue scenarios into headcount needs, and a rough outline of what the 2026 season looks like for each of you under three sales conditions framed around Labs outcomes specifically:

  • Condition 1 — Labs doesn't scale. Labs revenue lands at near zero ($0–0.5M). Most projects route through Nites residential and Tier C commercial. Labs ops contracts.
  • Condition 2 — Plan hits. Current 2026 plan baseline. Labs at ~$2M / ~12% mix. ~20 Labs projects (~50/50 between Tier A "full production" and Tier B "specialty pop-ups").
  • Condition 3 — Labs grows aggressively. Sales overperforms on Labs specifically — Gaylord expansion, municipal RFP wins, more Tier B closes. Labs at 20–25% mix ($3–4M).

Within each Labs condition, Nites revenue varies independently — so the org plan has to accommodate "Nites at $12M with Condition 1 Labs" as well as "Nites at $16M with Condition 3 Labs," and everything in between.

The goal of this meeting is to (a) align on the data we need to pull to make this model decision-useful and (b) sketch how each of our roles shifts as the season unfolds — so we know what to plan for, what to hedge against, and what to leave open until the season tells us which combination we're in.

Data Inquiries

What the Model Needs

The headcount model is only as good as the data feeding it. Below is what the model needs, what we have today, and what's still open.

1.A — The data the model depends on

The framework is built on a small set of inputs:

  • Per-crew throughput — $/crew/day, $/crew/season for Brite Nites exterior holiday work. Calibrates the formula for how much revenue each Nites field crew produces.
  • Labs project pipeline detail — project count, average duration by tier, geographic distribution. Drives the concurrency-based math for Labs supervisor demand.
  • Mix of revenue by tier — what portion of total sales is Tier A (full production, Labs Sup dedicated), Tier B (specialty commercial, Labs Sup required), Tier C (straightforward commercial, Nites motion).
  • Pipeline-stage forecasts — committed-deals-only and full-weighted-pipeline revenue, by record type and territory, so the model can output both a "floor" and "ceiling" headcount estimate.

Sales-Success Conditions

The Three Conditions

The 2026 plan calls for ~$16M install-season revenue split as ~$14M Nites + ~$2M Labs. Both numbers move. Brite Nites can land anywhere in the ~$12M–$16M+ range based on Nites sales performance; Brite Labs can land anywhere in the ~$0–$4M+ range based on Labs pipeline conversion. The three conditions below describe the Labs portion specifically — but Nites scale variation overlays on top of any condition.

The conditions are not equally likely. The middle one is the plan-hits scenario; the others are downside and upside contingencies for the Labs portion. The Headcount Formula in Section 4 takes both Nites scale and Labs mix as inputs and produces role counts that flex with each.

Condition 1 · Downside
Labs Doesn't Scale
Sales succeeds at the Nites side, but the Labs pipeline doesn't convert at the expected rate. Labs revenue lands at near zero ($0–0.5M, ~0–3% mix). Most commercial work routes through Tier C — straightforward outdoor lighting on commercial sites, executed by Nites motion. A handful of Tier B pop-ups may still close opportunistically but Tier A full-production work (Gaylord-scale) doesn't materialize.

What this looks like in the field: ~5 small Labs jobs all season. No need for a dedicated Labs producer hire. Existing leadership (Collin, Rainer, Erwin, AM backup) easily covers what little Labs supervision is needed.

Signal you'd see by: Late September. If the Tier A pipeline is empty heading into late September and the bar/restaurant Tier B inbound hasn't materialized, this is the condition we're operating in. The implication is significant: by the time we know we're in Condition 1, the install season has started. The Collin role pivot toward production development on the Nites side (Section 3) would emerge mid-season as Labs ops capacity becomes available, rather than being pre-planned.
Condition 2 · Plan Hits
Current 2026 Baseline
The published 2026 plan baseline. ~$14M Nites + ~$2M Labs = ~$16M total. Labs at ~12% mix. ~20 Labs projects across the season, split roughly 50/50 between Tier A (full production, ~$150K avg, Labs Supervisor dedicated 1–2 weeks per project) and Tier B (specialty commercial pop-ups, ~$50K avg, Labs Supervisor required but parallelizable at 2 jobs/week comfortable).

What this looks like in the field: Labs supervisor function covered by Collin + Rainer + Erwin + AM help, no new dedicated supervisor hire needed. Producer role (the formal Labs Supervisor hire) is on the table but optional for 2026; lean target is 3–4 Labs-exclusive year-round FTE total (current 2 + producer + maybe second sales rep).

Signal you'd see by: End of September. Tier A commitments locked in and Tier B inbound steady through the early-season window. By end of September the picture is clear enough to staff the rest of the season with confidence.
Condition 3 · Upside
Labs Grows Aggressively
Sales overperforms on Labs specifically. Gaylord expansion converts multiple properties; municipal RFP wins land; Tier B pop-ups close at 1.5–2× plan volume. Labs at 20–25% mix ($3–4M Labs revenue). ~35–40 Labs projects across the season; Tier A count alone doubles or more.

What this looks like in the field: Existing-leadership Labs supervisor coverage tops out. Need at peak 3–4 dedicated Labs Supervisors; leadership-team capacity (Collin + Rainer + Erwin) can no longer absorb the concurrency. Producer hire becomes a definite, not optional; second producer or AE may also be triggered. Equipment fleet planning becomes acute (more MEWPs / scissor lifts deployed simultaneously). AM cross-training into Labs supervision becomes a real lever.

Signal you'd see by: August. When the early-locking Tier A pipeline is visible. Lead time for a producer hire is real — if Condition 3 is materializing, the producer search would ideally have been kicked off by May/June, but August is the latest the org can commit to a Condition 3 staffing posture and still react in time.

The Headcount Formula

Translating Revenue Scenarios into Role Counts

The model decomposes total revenue into Nites and Labs portions and produces headcount needs by role. Both Nites scale and Labs mix are independent inputs. The formula scales linearly with Nites revenue and adds Labs-specific roles (supervisors + producer + specialty) based on Labs project count and concurrency.

4.A — The formula

Inputs:
  R_n = Brite Nites revenue (~$12M–$16M+ range)
  R_l = Brite Labs revenue (~$0–$4M+ range)
  Labs project mix (count of Tier A jobs, count of Tier B jobs)

Brite Nites field heads:
  Total Nites field = R_n / I_n
    where I_n = $144K per FTE per season
    (calibrated from 2025 actuals + 2026 plan; needs Mike's validation)
  Split per 2-person crew: 50% Crew Leaders + 50% Installers

Labs Supervisors (concurrency-driven, not revenue-driven):
  Tier A demand = N_A × avg_duration_A_weeks (1 supervisor per project, dedicated)
  Tier B demand = N_B × duration_B_days / (5 × 2)
    (1 supervisor handles ~2 Tier B per week comfortably)
  Peak concurrent = (Tier A + Tier B demand / season_weeks) × peak_multiplier (~1.5)
  Labs Supervisors needed = Peak concurrent

Labs-exclusive year-round (step function):
  2 FTE at Labs < $1M (Collin + Rainer)
  3–4 FTE at Labs $1–3M (+ producer + maybe sales rep)
  +1 producer per ~$2M of Labs growth above $3M

Total org = Nites field + Labs Supervisors + Labs year-round exclusives. Rough end-to-end view across the most-common scale × mix combinations:

Totals approximate. Scale (Nites revenue) drives magnitude; mix (Labs share) primarily shifts role composition (more Labs supervisors / specialty + fewer pure-Nites bodies at the margin). The 2025 finding — utilization matters more than headcount — still applies inside any cell.
